Your messages, photos, videos and payments are private, and you don't need a phone number or email to use Polkadot. Some things do involve other services, and this article lists them so you know where they are. 

## What's private

- Every message is end-to-end encrypted. Only you and the person you're chatting with can read it.
- Photos and videos are encrypted before they leave your phone and sent via the chat. 
- Voice and video calls are encrypted and peer-to-peer.
- Every payment is private, and your payments aren't linked to your username.
- Messages are delivered over Polkadot's public network, not a company server.
- No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no IP logging.

## What other people can see

Your username is registered on Polkadot so that people can find you, which means anyone can search for it. People you chat with see your username.

If a chat shows "This chat can't be fully private right now" and you tap **Use username**, some activity in that chat, like when you send messages, may be linked to your username. 
See ["This chat can't be fully private right now"](../03-chats-and-calls/chat-not-fully-private.md).

## Where other services are involved

- When you add or cash out ᴄᴀꜱʜ by card or bank transfer, the payment provider checks your ID, so it knows who paid and how much. Crypto on Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana and Tron goes through Chainflip, an exchange service. Once it's in, your ᴄᴀꜱʜ is private.
- To suggest a payment country when you add or cash out ᴄᴀꜱʜ, the app looks up your country from your internet connection, using another service.
- On the Google Play version, device verification uses Google Play Integrity, a Google service, to check your phone is genuine. The direct download version uses a check built into your phone instead.
- On the Google Play version, your account backup is saved to your Google Drive, encrypted.
- The Polkadot Community Foundation (PCF), which publishes the app, runs some services the app needs, including username search, device verification, helping calls connect, push notifications and delivering encrypted photos and videos. Google's servers also help calls connect.
