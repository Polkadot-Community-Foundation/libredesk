Scams usually go after one of two things: your recovery phrase, or a payment you can't get back. Payments can't be reversed once the other person has received them, so it helps to know the usual tricks.

## Fake support

Someone sends you a message request saying they're from Polkadot support or the Polkadot team. They say there's a problem with your account, then ask for your recovery phrase or for ᴄᴀꜱʜ to fix it.

Polkadot support will never ask for either, and we only contact people by email, from help@pcf.network. Block them, and see [How Polkadot support contacts you](how-support-contacts-you.md).

## Requests for your recovery phrase

A person, a website or another app asks you to enter your recovery phrase to "verify", "sync" or "unlock" your account. Anyone who has your recovery phrase can open your account and spend your ᴄᴀꜱʜ. See [Someone asked for my recovery phrase](someone-asked-for-recovery-phrase.md).

## "Recovery services" that charge a fee

After something has gone wrong, someone offers to get your ᴄᴀꜱʜ or your account back if you pay them first. Nobody can do this. Payments can't be reversed, and without your recovery phrase or backup nobody can open your account, including Polkadot. These offers often target people who have recently lost ᴄᴀꜱʜ to a scam.

## Lookalike usernames

A scammer takes a friend's name with different digits, for example jake.32 instead of jake.23, and asks for ᴄᴀꜱʜ. They often say they've lost their phone or started a new account. A friend who restores their account on a new phone keeps the same username. See [Check who you're chatting with before you pay](check-who-youre-paying.md).

## "I sent you too much, please send it back"

Someone says they paid you by mistake and asks you to return the difference, sometimes with a screenshot as proof. Often nothing arrived at all.

Open your chat with them and look at the payment. Only ᴄᴀꜱʜ marked "Received" is yours. A payment that still says "is sending you" hasn't arrived yet. If you really did receive too much, send back exactly the extra amount in the same chat, and nothing more.

## Offers to sell you ᴄᴀꜱʜ

Someone offers ᴄᴀꜱʜ at a discount, or asks you to pay them by bank transfer, card or crypto and promises to send ᴄᴀꜱʜ in return. Add ᴄᴀꜱʜ only with **Get** in your Pocket, where the payment details come from the app itself. See [How to get ᴄᴀꜱʜ](../05-get-and-send-to-yourself/ways-to-get-cash.md).

## Promises to grow your ᴄᴀꜱʜ

Someone you've been chatting with, sometimes for weeks, promises to double your ᴄᴀꜱʜ or offers returns if you send some first. Anyone who promises to grow your ᴄᴀꜱʜ is running a scam. Don't send anything, and block them.

## If you've already paid a scammer

Stop sending ᴄᴀꜱʜ, even if they promise to return it once you pay a little more. Payments can't be reversed once the other person has received them, and Polkadot support can't take ᴄᴀꜱʜ back for you.

1. Block them: in the chat, tap **⋮** > **Block user**.
2. If you shared your recovery phrase, follow [Someone asked for my recovery phrase](someone-asked-for-recovery-phrase.md).
3. Email [help@pcf.network](mailto:help@pcf.network) and tell us what happened.

Anyone who then offers to get your ᴄᴀꜱʜ back for a fee is running another scam.
